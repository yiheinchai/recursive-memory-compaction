07  Dreaming

rmc dream — whole-store consolidation, independent of any session. Every other path reacts to the session in front of it; this one steps back.

It grows abstraction from co-use: lessons repeatedly used together on work that succeeded. That signal is the right one because the pair most worth merging often shares no surface features — a lesson about deployment tooling and one about cache economics can belong under one abstraction purely because they are needed together, and no similarity metric will place them near each other.

Counting co-occurrence is evidence and stays in code. Whether a group shares a generalisable idea is the model's call. Threshold is 2 occurrences; one is coincidence. Failed sessions do not count.

When it runs

Dreaming is not a reaction to a session, so no session event is its natural occasion. It runs on elapsed time gated by new evidence: at most once per dream.interval_s (24h), and then on either of two signals.

Co-use is the strong one — lessons repeatedly used together on work that succeeded. But recall serves about one lesson per prompt, so episodes using two at once are rare, and a store can run for a month without producing a single co-use group. On its own it leaves the long tail flat.

Width is the fallback. Every top-level lesson is enumerated on every prompt, so once there are more than dream.max_apexes (12), the model is asked to group them whether or not they have ever met. Weaker evidence, but not unchecked: the merged parent still has to reproduce every child’s episodes, and still has to come in under compaction.merge_ratio of their combined size — a parent no smaller than what it stands in front of makes every prompt more expensive, which is the opposite of why you merged.

All of those gates are structural — a clock reading and three counts — so none belongs to the model. It is invoked from absorb, the detached end-of-session pipeline, because that is the one place that already runs off the main thread, already holds a lock, and is already allowed to spend calls. Most sessions skip it in a line.

Seeing what it did

A dream rewrites the store while nobody is watching, which is exactly the situation that needs a record. Every pass writes a report to .rmc/dreams/: 

# dream 2026-08-16T16:12:41Z

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| nodes | 12 | 13 |
| apexes | 9 | 8 |
| tokens served at apex | 2088 | 1640 |

Examined 3 co-use group(s); wrote 2 gist(s).

## merged
- n_cache+n_deploy (co-used 4x) -> n_9f10

## refused
- n_a+n_b (co-used 3x): merge regression pass-rate 67% < 100%

tokens served at apex is the number that matters — it is what recall pays on every prompt. 

rmc dream --due        # is a dream due, and why not
rmc dream --list       # merge candidates from co-use, change nothing
rmc dream --log        # the last dream's report
rmc dream --log all    # every dream on record
rmc dream --dry-run    # generate and validate, write nothing
